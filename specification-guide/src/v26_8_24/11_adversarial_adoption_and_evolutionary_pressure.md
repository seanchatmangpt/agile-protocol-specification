# Adversarial Adoption and Evolutionary Pressure

A successor production model can be economically rational at the system level while being individually irrational for many incumbent participants to adopt early.

That is not merely a communications problem. It is part of the selection environment in which the architecture must evolve.

## Why rational incumbents can resist

The incumbent system embeds capital in:

- specialized human skills;
- organizational roles;
- review hierarchies;
- vendor relationships;
- certification processes;
- architecture conventions;
- risk models;
- status and identity;
- sunk training and platform investment.

A manufacturing system that moves knowledge into public semantics, contracts, jigs, and verifier law can depreciate several of those assets simultaneously.

From the enterprise objective function the successor may dominate. From an individual's local objective function, opposing it may be rational.

## Adoption barrier as evolutionary pressure

APS therefore does not treat resistance as proof of misunderstanding. Resistance is a source of adversarial requirements.

```text
adversarial objection
  -> candidate falsifier
  -> explicit failure state
  -> architecture/test/evidence response
  -> stronger successor or admitted limit
```

The best critics often optimize for a different loss function and therefore expose states the constructor never searched.

## The liability challenge

Questions such as the following are architecture-level tests:

- What happens when a manufactured consequence causes catastrophic harm?
- Who possessed authority for the consequential action?
- Which contract and policy version governed it?
- Can the exact decision and execution chain be reconstructed years later?
- What if every procedural step conformed but the process itself was unsafe?
- What if manufactured volume makes human artifact review impossible?
- What if the model, session, agent, or vendor no longer exists when accountability is demanded?

These are not arguments for returning to artisanal software. They are requirements for attributable authority, independent evidence, process fitness, replay, long-horizon provenance, and governance compression.

## Red Queen sequence

As lower objections are solved, criticism migrates upward:

```text
can it generate?
  -> is it correct?
  -> is verification independent?
  -> who authorized execution?
  -> is the authorized process fit?
  -> who is accountable?
  -> can the evidence survive regulation, litigation, and time?
```

This sequence is useful. Each migration indicates that a lower layer is becoming commoditized and the remaining competitive frontier has moved.

## Game-theoretic framing

Let the constructor choose a manufacturing architecture `m` and an adversary choose a plausible failure state `f`.

```text
Constructor payoff: qualified consequence, throughput, reversibility, capital accumulation
Adversary payoff: discover f such that claimedStanding(m, f) is false
```

A robust architecture maximizes consequence while making credible adversarial wins increasingly expensive to produce.

The goal is not to defeat people. The goal is to make unmodeled failure classes progressively rarer and more explicit.

## Do not persuade around missing evidence

If an objection reveals a missing authority path, verifier, replay contract, process-fitness test, or semantic closure proof, rhetoric is not a substitute.

The successor earns adoption by making the disputed proposition observable:

```text
does the successor dominate the incumbent process
on admitted objectives, constraints, and evidence?
```

Where it does not, APS records the boundary instead of declaring inevitability.

## Adversaries become part of the factory

A mature manufacturing system should capture successful attacks as reusable candidate falsifiers. Over time, yesterday's criticism becomes tomorrow's automatic qualification gate.

Adversarial pressure therefore compounds manufacturing capital rather than merely slowing adoption.
