# Software Manufacturing as CapEx

Conventional software accounting and management often treat development throughput as recurring labor expense. APS treats reusable manufacturing capability differently: a qualified ontology, contract, jig, template, verifier, generator composition, or factory improvement can increase the future output capacity of the entire system.

That is a capital-equipment perspective.

## From programmer effort to manufacturing capacity

The conventional question is:

```text
How much work did the team complete?
```

The manufacturing question is:

```text
How much qualified production capacity was added to the factory?
```

A hand-authored feature can be consumed once. A reusable manufacturing pattern can produce a family of future consequences.

## Manufacturing units

Commits, files, tests, schemas, docs, generated lines, packages, and deployed resources can all be useful manufacturing units, but none is the final objective by itself.

APS distinguishes:

```text
output volume
qualified output volume
reusable manufacturing capital created
novel human knowledge required
```

The maximization target is not fewest commits or smallest diff. DfCM can deliberately manufacture many reversible candidates, falsifiers, tests, projections, and documentation surfaces because compute changes the economics of exploration.

## Combinatorial maximalism

Efficiency language often assumes scarce human production is the dominant constraint and therefore prunes options early. DfCM inverts that when manufacture and qualification are cheap enough:

```text
maximize lawful reversible candidate space
subject to authority, resource, evidence, and qualification constraints
```

This can produce more artifacts than a conventional team would consider rational. The point is not artifact vanity. The point is to move design-space search from scarce human labor into reproducible manufacturing and verification.

## Capital accumulation

Useful capital includes:

- public ontology profiles;
- enterprise ABox/contract knowledge;
- manufacturing packs and patterns;
- jigs and fixtures;
- generator adapters;
- verifier courts;
- process-intelligence mappings;
- authority policies;
- replay/evidence infrastructure;
- reconstitution knowledge.

A successful novel mechanism should increase this stock rather than remain a one-off patch.

## Manufacturing rate metrics

Useful factory measures include:

```text
qualified artifacts / hour
qualified candidate variants / hour
contracts admitted / hour
reusable patterns promoted / hour
independent falsifiers manufactured / hour
first-pass qualification rate
replay stability
manufacturing multiplier
handwritten residual ratio
```

A particularly revealing ratio is:

```text
Reuse Multiplier = future qualified consequences enabled / novel mechanism authored
```

## Commit maximalism with claim discipline

A high commit count can be a legitimate manufacturing strategy when commits are non-vacuous, independently useful, reversible units that expand capability, evidence, tests, docs, patterns, or qualification surfaces.

But commit count does not prove quality or economic value. The factory should be able to explain what production capability or evidence each unit adds.

The target is **maximum qualified manufacture**, not minimum work and not meaningless churn.

## CapEx does not exempt the factory from depreciation

Chatman's Law applies to the capital equipment too. Today's manufacturing machinery becomes sunk when a better qualified manufacturing system can reconstitute its useful knowledge and outperform it.

The durable asset is therefore not any particular generator implementation. It is the recoverable manufacturing knowledge and evidence that allows the factory itself to be replaced.
