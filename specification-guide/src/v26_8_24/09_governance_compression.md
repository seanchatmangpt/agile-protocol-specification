# Governance Compression and Board-Level Validation

The governance consequence of semantic manufacture is not that oversight disappears. It moves upstream.

## Validate manufacturing law, not every artifact

A Fortune-500 board or delegated governing authority cannot inspect millions of generated consequences. A viable manufacturing system therefore separates qualification of the production law from derivation of individual outputs.

The governing set is approximately:

```text
semantic authority
+ contracts
+ manufacturing templates/generators
+ policy/authority law
+ verifier law
```

Once a version of that set is admitted, downstream artifacts may inherit standing only when they prove derivation from the admitted coordinate and independently satisfy required qualification.

'Validate once' means **once per admitted version**, not forever. A change to source semantics, template, generator, policy, runtime boundary, or verifier creates a new candidate manufacturing coordinate that must earn standing.

## Governance reuse ratio

Define:

```text
G = qualified downstream consequences / human governance decisions
```

Traditional enterprise change often has low `G` because each application, project, integration, deployment, control, and release creates additional review activity.

Semantic manufacturing seeks high `G` without weakening evidence.

## Board-level responsibilities

The board should increasingly govern questions such as:

- What must be true?
- What must never occur?
- Which semantic definitions are authoritative?
- Which objectives and risk limits apply?
- Who or what may authorize consequential action?
- What evidence constitutes standing?
- What conditions require refusal, escalation, rollback, or sunset?

The board should not need to validate generated controller code, Kubernetes YAML, API wrappers, mappings, or test fixtures line by line when those are qualified projections of admitted law.

## Constitution versus consequence

APS distinguishes constitutional authority from manufactured consequence:

```text
governing authority
  -> admits meaning, constraints, objectives, authority, evidence law
  -> manufacturing system derives operational consequences
  -> independent verification assigns standing
  -> exceptions return to governance
```

This is analogous to qualifying production equipment, fixtures, gauges, and process law rather than personally inspecting every manufactured component.

## Governance must survive combinatorial output

DfCM may intentionally construct many variants. Therefore any governance model whose human review requirement grows linearly with candidate or artifact count defeats the manufacturing objective.

Governance must instead be based on:

- bounded contracts;
- reusable qualification law;
- deterministic/provenance-bound derivation where claimed;
- independent falsifiers;
- evidence aggregation;
- exception routing.

## Project-to-knowledge transition

The historical unit of corporate change is often the project. Under APS the preferred unit becomes an admitted change to enterprise knowledge or manufacturing law:

```text
Delta semantics / contract / policy / objective
  -> candidate remanufacture
  -> qualification
  -> bounded release
  -> observed process consequences
```

Projects may continue to exist where useful, but receive no constitutional status.
